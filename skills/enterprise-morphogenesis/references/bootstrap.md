# Bootstrap: minimum viable loop

Do this before any enterprise-wide operating model.

## Preconditions

- A domain with a **willing owner**
- One **real obligation** (regulatory, customer, safety, or money)
- Authority that is at least **local** (Section 12: architecture without authority fails)

If those are missing, say so. Do not simulate a loop with fictional data presented as production.

## Build, in order

1. **Name the intended form** for that domain (become, remain, or retire a behavior).
2. **Seed a graph slice**: only entities the obligation touches (people/teams, products/services, data, systems, agents, physical assets if they matter, the obligation itself).
3. **Encode 2-3 invariants** as checks in the path of work (pipeline, policy decision point, agent tool, runtime gate). Not a PDF.
4. **Bind one sensing signal** to graph positions (incident, cost, cycle time, exception, model confidence, safety event).
5. **Switch provenance on**: every exception has intent, authority, evidence, expiry. Distinguish trajectory exceptions from drift exceptions.
6. **Measure** architecture latency on that slice, and whether the slice is inside its viable region.
7. **Teach the Atlas page** for that slice to the humans who work there.

Stop. Do not add domains until this loop is consumed and both sides of dials 1 and 2 move.

## Growth rule

The first closed loop is an organizer. Adjacent domains join by connecting to it, not by converting to a method. Sequencing is local. Do not publish a 12-month maturity roadmap.

## Explicitly forbidden at bootstrap

- Company-wide ontology programme as the first move
- Capability-map recertification
- Architecture board redesign as the first move
- A single composite "morphogenesis score"
- Buying a graph database before anything consumes a graph
